# GameDay Venue pilot usability script

Use this with a Venue GM and at least one frontline Venue Staff member on their own phones. Do not teach the product before the session. Ask the participant to think aloud; do not answer the tasks for them.

Target length: 25–35 minutes. Use a clearly marked disposable pilot venue and restore any operational changes afterward.

## Opening

“We are testing the product, not you. Please tell me what you expect before each tap. If something is confusing, keep going as you naturally would.”

## Venue GM tasks

1. “You just arrived. Tell me what is happening right now.”
2. “Show me which fields need attention.”
3. “Find what is currently happening on Field 7.”
4. “Show me what you would do if Field 7 had to close. Stop before confirming a destructive change.”
5. “Find a future game and show me where you would change it.”
6. “Find an active Work Order.”
7. “Assign or review ownership of a Work Order.”
8. “Publish an operational announcement for this test venue.”
9. “Show me the overall venue status.”

## Venue Staff tasks

1. “Tell me what is happening now.”
2. “Find Field 7.”
3. “Identify a field that needs attention.”
4. “Find a Work Order assigned to you.”
5. “Claim or acknowledge work where applicable.”
6. “Start the work.”
7. “Resolve it with a useful note.”
8. “Find the latest announcement.”

## Public check

“Pretend you are a parent who scanned the Field 7 QR code. Tell me what is happening now and next.”

## Failure prompts

Use test-safe network throttling or a prepared failure; never ask the participant to change real production data.

1. “Try that field update again after the connection fails. What do you think happened?”
2. “Another operator changed this item first. What would you do now?”
3. “Use Back to return to where you started. Is your context still clear?”

## Observe without coaching

- Where did they hesitate or stop?
- Did they choose the wrong destination first?
- Did Home, Today, Fields, Schedule, Work Orders, and Venue Status mean what they expected?
- Did they find the correct action without coaching?
- Did they notice and understand success, failure, permission, and conflict feedback?
- Did they trust that the displayed state was current?
- Could they use the controls with one hand outdoors?
- Did text, status, or buttons wrap or clip at their text-size setting?
- How many purposeful taps did each task take?
- How long did each task take?

## Close

Ask only after all tasks:

1. “What felt easiest?”
2. “What felt risky or unclear?”
3. “What would you expect to do here that you could not find?”
4. “Which screen would you leave open during a normal shift?”
5. “Would you trust this during a busy game day? Why or why not?”

Record behavior and exact participant language. Separate observed problems from feature requests; do not promise a solution during the session.
